## KI KUR KI JAIT

Ki Khun ki ksiew u Hynñiew Trep Hynñiew Skum ki long kiba riewspah ha ka liang ki kur ki jait. Kine ki jait ki khong ki wan da ka daw bad la pynskhem ïa ki da ka niam tynrai U Hynñiew Trep. Ngi ngeit ba ki kur ki jait ki mih na ka Ïawbei tynrai kaba long ka kmie nyngkong jong kawei pa kawei ka kur ha ka jait bynriew Hynñiew Trep. U shongkha ba nyngkong eh jong ka kur la khot kyrteng u Thawlang bad ia u longkñi nyngkong la ong u Suitnia.

Ngi burom ia ka longkur ka longkha, bad la bynshet ia ka kamram ka longkmie, u longkñi bad u longkpa ha ka rukom long ïieng longsem jong ngi pateng la pateng. Ki kynthei Hynñiew Trep ki ri ia ka jait ka khong ki kmie bad kñi tymmen naduh nyngkong ka sngi. Ki dei ki longkmie ha ki khun ki kti bad ki longkha ha ki khun ki para shynrang ne hymmen shynrang jong ki. U Rangbah Hynñiew Trep u long u longkñi ha ki pyrsa para ha ïiengkur ha kaba ka spah ka phew bad ka synshar ïieng ka synshar kur ka don hangta. Ki Hirna Khasi ki bat ia kane ka dustur nylla ha kaba u pyrsa shynrang u shimti ia ka synshar khadar ynda khyllait u kñi. Ha ïiengkhun u shynrang Hynñiewtrep u long u longkpa ha kaba ka jingieid bad sumar sukher
ka shong ha ki tyrpeng jong u.

Ki kur ki jait u Hynñiew Trep ki ïai heh kur lyngba ki kynthei na ki jait, ki kpoh bad ki ïieng. Bunsien ngi lap ba ka kur kaba heh jaid ka don bun tylli ki shnat kur ha ki jait ba iapher. Kane ka long na ka daw ka jingkynriah shnong ne iieng bad la tip noh ia ki da kawei pat ka kyrteng jait. Hynrei katba dang tip ia ka snam kur,
kata ba iamih na kajuh ka ïawbei tynrai, ka mynsiem ieid kur ka synshar ha ki. Shikur ki long shidoh shisnam bad ka ’lei longkur long jait ka kdup ia ki.

## Summary
    **Sacred Origin of the Clans (Ki Kur ki Jait)**

    The Hynñiew Trep (Khasi people) possess a rich heritage of clans, which originated with a purpose and are sanctified by the indigenous Khasi faith (ka niam tynrai).

    Every clan traces its lineage back to three foundational ancestors:

        - **Ka Ïawbei Tynrai:** The primal root-mother or first ancestress of the clan.

        - **U Thawlang:** The first progenitor/father who married into the clan (u shongkha ba nyngkong).

        - **U Suitnia:** The first maternal uncle (u longkñi nyngkong) who served as the spiritual mediator and advisor.

    **Roles and Responsibilities in Family & Society**

    Khasi society upholds deep reverence for both maternal ties (ka longkur) and paternal/in-law ties (ka longkha), assigning distinct, complementary duties across generations:

        - **Women (Ki Kynthei):** Preserve and carry forward the clan surname (ka jait ka khong) from ancient times. They serve as mothers (longkmie) to their own children and as paternal aunts (longkha) to the children of their brothers.

        - **Men as Maternal Uncles (U Longkñi):** In their maternal clan home (ïingkur), men hold administrative, economic, and clan authority over their sisters' children (ki pyrsa). Among traditional Hima Khasi (Khasi states), this principle extends to governance, where a nephew (pyrsa shynrang) succeeds his maternal uncle in leadership.

        - **Men as Fathers (U Longkpa):** In their marital home (ïingkhun), men fulfill the role of father, carrying the responsibility of providing love, care, and protection for their children.

    **Clan Expansion and Unbreakable Unity**

    Clans grow and branch out through women across generations—expanding from the household (ïing), to the womb-lineage (kpoh), to the broader clan (kur).

    Over time, migration and relocation often cause large clans to divide into sub-clans (shnat kur) adopting distinct surnames.

    Regardless of different surnames, as long as members recognize their shared bloodline descending from the same Ïawbei Tynrai, they remain bound by a spirit of kinship—considered one flesh and one blood (shidoh shisnam) under the embrace of the guardian spirit of clanhood (ka ’lei longkur long jait).

## Rukom Shongkha Shongman
Shaphang ka shongkha shongman, u Hynñiew Trep u bat kyntang ïaka. Um ju shongkha shikur ne shipara kha. Dang lap ha kine ki snem ba hadien ba kano kano ka jait bynriew kaba shongkha jan than haba dang don jingïadei jan ha ka snam, kam juh lah neh slem. Ki khun ki kti jong ki, la ong, ki long anna ha ka met ka phad bad ki long kiba duna ba ka liang ka pyrkhat ka pyrdaiñ.

Ka rukom pyrkhat shaphang ka ïawbei ne kmie kur ka long kaba la ïaw ne slem bad la buh jingmut bha shaphang jong ka da ki longdien. Ngi shem ba tang shu kha ia ki khyllung shynrang ki jer ki thoh da ki khnam ïawbei, ryntieh ïawbei bad la ki kynthei pat da ka wait ka khoh ïawbei. Ki jer bad kyrteng ia ki khyllung ha ka jait jong ka kmie. Lada i khyllung i iam kyndit haba thiah khlem tip briew ei ei ki
ju ong mai ka ïawbei. Hynrei lada i rkhie haba ïohthiah ki ju ong iaraiñ ka ïawbei. Wat ia ka longrynieng haduh u shñiuh ki bniat la ngeit ki dei ki jong ka ïawbei. Ha kane ka rukom sa ioh kynnoh ki ’niat iawbei katba ym pat khein ki bniat nyngkong, bad ki ’ñiuh iawbei ha shynrong khlieh ki khyllung katba ym pat palat shi snem
naduh ba kha.

U shynrang Hynñiew Trep um ju shim jait shalade. Dei ki kynthei kiba bat ia ka jait. Ha ki sngi ba kham hadien don bun bah ki shynrang Hynñiew Trep kiba shongkha ha ki kynthei dkhar ai jait kpa, pynban kim shym pynbakla ia ka dustur kheiñ kur kheiñ kha. U kyntang ïawbei ia ka bad ki khun jong u, ki ioh ia ki jait thymmai ha ka jait bynriew Hynñiew Trep.


## Summary 

    **Sanctity of Marriage & Strict Exogamy (Ka Shongkha Shongman)**

        - The Hynñiew Trep hold marriage as sacred (bat kyntang).
        - Marriage within the same clan (shongkha shikur) or between close paternal kin (shipara kha) is strictly forbidden.
        - The text notes that marrying close blood relatives leads to the decline of a community over time, resulting in physical ailments (anna ha ka met ka phad) and diminished mental faculties (duna ha ka liang ka pyrkhat ka pyrdaiñ) in offspring.

    **The Primal Ancestress (Ka Ïawbei) in Naming & Infancy**

    Reverence for Ka Ïawbei (the maternal clan root) is deeply embedded in childhood traditions:

        - **Naming Ceremony (Jer Thoh)**: Immediately after birth, a boy is named using the khnam ïawbei and ryntieh ïawbei (ancestral bow and arrows), while a girl is named using the wait and khoh ïawbei (ancestral knife and conical basket). Both receive their mother's clan surname (jait).

        - **Spiritual Bond in Infancy**: If a sleeping baby suddenly cries without apparent cause, elders say Ka Ïawbei is scolding them (mai ka ïawbei); if the baby smiles in their sleep, Ka Ïawbei is playing with or amusing them (iaraiñ ka ïawbei).

        - **Physical Attribution**: A baby's physical build, first milk teeth (’niat ïawbei), and infant hair (’ñiuh ïawbei, before their first year) are all believed to belong to Ka Ïawbei.

    **Matrilineal Surnames & Cross-Cultural Marriages**

        A Khasi man never passes his own surname to his children; women are the sole custodians and carriers of the jait.

        Even in later times when many Khasi men married non-Khasi women (kynthei dkhar) from patrilineal societies, they did not violate the fundamental Khasi code of kinship (kheiñ kur kheiñ kha).

        Instead, through traditional consecration (kyntang ïawbei), the non-Khasi wife is formally honored as a primal ancestress (ïawbei), allowing their children to receive a distinct new clan surname (jait thymmai) within the Hynñiew Trep fold.
